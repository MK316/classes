Read Like a Researcher

Goal Move beyond summarizing the paper and examine the relationship between the research question, evidence, and conclusion.
You are not simply reporting someone else's paper.
You are learning to understand the study from the inside.

Three Questions for Critical Reading

Claim
What does the paper claim?
Evidence
What evidence supports the claim?
Fit
Does the evidence justify the claim?
Critical reading does not mean finding faults.
It means examining how well the research question, evidence, and conclusion fit together.

3

Think Like a Reviewer

Goal Distinguish issues that affect the validity or interpretation of a study from smaller editorial problems.

Review the Same Hypothetical Manuscript

Topic: Generative AI-based feedback in university English writing

  • 42 university students in one general English class
  • 4 weeks of generative AI-assisted writing and revision
  • pre- and post-writing tasks scored on content, organization, vocabulary, and grammar
  • 12-item perception survey and open-ended reflections
  • post-writing scores were significantly higher than pre-writing scores
Key claim to examine:
The manuscript interprets the improvement as evidence that generative AI has a direct effect on students' English writing ability and can improve independent writing performance.
Reopen Manuscript (PDF)

Review the Study

  1. Identify the single most serious problem in the manuscript.
  2. Write one major comment that explains why the problem matters for the study's conclusion.
  3. Write one minor comment on reporting, wording, tables, or references.
  4. Choose: Accept / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Reject.
  5. Explain your decision briefly.
  6. As a group, agree on one final editorial decision.

Major Issues

  • RQ and analysis do not align
  • conclusion is unsupported by the data
  • important variable was not measured
  • method cannot answer the RQ
  • causal claim from non-causal evidence

Minor Issues

  • unclear wording
  • inconsistent terminology
  • formatting problems
  • table labels
  • citation/reference errors
Discuss:
Is the problem fixable?
Does it affect the main conclusion?
Would revision be enough?
What is the single most important revision needed?
4

Prepare Your Own Presentation

Goal Apply today's framework directly to the paper you will present next week.

Work with Your Selected Paper

  1. Write the research problem in one sentence.
  2. Write the research gap in one sentence.
  3. Identify the main RQ(s).
  4. Summarize the method in no more than four bullet points.
  5. Select only two findings that are essential for answering the RQ(s).
  6. Write one implication.
  7. Identify the strongest point and the most vulnerable point.
  8. Prepare two likely audience questions.
Research Problem
This study addresses...
Research Gap
We know..., but we still do not know...
Two Key Findings
Choose findings that answer the RQs.
Main Implication
The findings suggest that...
Strongest Point
What would you defend confidently?
Most Vulnerable Point
What might the audience question first?
